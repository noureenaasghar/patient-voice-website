The initiative

Making medication access a shared research priority.

For people living with chronic conditions, access to long-term medicines can be affected by more than whether a medicine is available. Cost, distance, communication, treatment burden, and fragmented support may all shape the experience. This concept places patients and communities at the center of understanding those challenges.

Research overview

Medication access is a journey, not a single event.

For people managing long-term conditions, getting a medicine is only one part of access. People may need to find an affordable option, travel to a source, understand instructions, obtain refills on time, and navigate changes in their treatment or health system.

What the literature tells us

Barriers are connected

Research on access to medicines for chronic conditions has identified affordability and availability as recurring concerns. Distance, transportation costs, health-system arrangements, and socioeconomic circumstances can also shape whether people obtain and continue their medicines.

Why patient perspectives matter

Numbers do not tell the whole story

Understanding a barrier also means understanding how it affects daily decisions, relationships with healthcare providers, treatment routines, and the ability to continue care. Patients and caregivers can help identify which problems and outcomes matter most to them.

Proposed research problem

How can medication access research move from describing barriers to prioritizing patient-defined needs and developing locally responsive strategies?

This is the problem this concept seeks to explore. The precise setting, condition groups, and scope will be narrowed after a more focused literature review and assessment of feasibility.

Central research question

How can patients, caregivers, community pharmacists, and community organizations collaboratively identify medication access barriers, prioritize their needs, and co-design patient-centered strategies to improve access and continuity of care?

A cautious statement of the research gap

Existing research has described multiple barriers to medication access, and patient-centered programs have developed approaches for engaging communities in health research. The opportunity explored here is to bring these strands together around a focused medication-access question: how affected people define and rank the barriers that matter in their own lives, and how those priorities can inform practical strategies developed with community pharmacists and local partners. This is a proposed direction, not a claim that no similar study exists. Its originality must be tested through a structured review of the literature and relevant ongoing projects.

Research aims

From listening to practical priorities.

The proposed work is organized around three connected aims. These will be refined through a focused literature review and discussion of existing evidence.

01

Understand lived experience

Explore how adults with chronic conditions and their caregivers experience barriers to obtaining and continuing prescribed medicines.

02

Prioritize what matters

Bring patient, caregiver, and community perspectives together to identify which barriers and outcomes deserve greatest attention.

03

Co-design responsive strategies

Work with community partners and pharmacists to develop practical, patient-informed approaches that can be assessed for feasibility.

Meaningful patient partnership

People are partners — not just participants.

The proposed approach values different forms of knowledge and creates opportunities for patients and community stakeholders to influence research priorities and potential solutions.

Proposed methodology

Listen first. Prioritize together. Test what is practical.

An exploratory sequential mixed-methods design is proposed: qualitative exploration will inform collaborative priority-setting and development of a practical strategy. This is a starting framework, not a finalized protocol.

Phase 01 · Explore

Understand experiences

Semi-structured interviews with adults living with chronic conditions, caregivers, and community pharmacists. Explore affordability, medicine availability, travel, refill interruptions, communication, treatment burden, and continuity of care.

Phase 02 · Prioritize

Make sense together

Thematic analysis will organize interview findings. Accessible summaries can be shared with patient partners to discuss and rank priority barriers, explain what matters, and co-design a practical response with community stakeholders.

Phase 03 · Assess

Examine feasibility

A small feasibility assessment of a selected co-designed strategy will focus on acceptability, practicality, usability, participant experience, and perceived value — not clinical effectiveness.

Potential outputs: patient-informed medication-access priorities, a collaboratively developed intervention concept, and preliminary feasibility findings. Population, condition focus, setting, recruitment, sample size, instruments, and analysis plan must be refined with academic supervision, community partners, and ethics review.

Potential contribution

From patient priorities to more responsive research.

The intended contribution is to strengthen how medication-access challenges are understood and addressed with the people who experience them.

01

Patient-informed priorities

Clarify which access barriers and outcomes patients and caregivers consider most important in daily life.

02

Stronger partnerships

Create meaningful opportunities for patients, caregivers, pharmacists, and community organizations to shape research decisions.

03

Practical next steps

Generate a co-designed strategy and preliminary feasibility insights to guide future research, service development, or policy discussion.

These are anticipated contributions, not guaranteed outcomes. Whether a strategy improves access or health outcomes would require subsequent evaluation in appropriately designed studies.
